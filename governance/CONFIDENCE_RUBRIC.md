# Claim Confidence Rubric

## Rating labels

| Label | Operational meaning |
|---|---|
| High | The claim is directly tested by multiple reasonably unbiased and precise studies, including independent validation in relevant settings. Important residual uncertainty would be unlikely to reverse the central conclusion. |
| Moderate | The central claim is supported, but limitations in directness, bias, precision, consistency, or transportability could materially change its magnitude or boundaries. |
| Low | Evidence is sparse, indirect, small, selected, inconsistent, or substantially biased. The claim is useful mainly as a working hypothesis. |
| Very low | Evidence is primarily speculative, anecdotal, or based on a model whose relevance to the human claim is uncertain. |
| Not rated | The record is definitional, methodological, or insufficiently extracted for synthesis. |

## Dimension scores

Each dimension is scored `0`, `1`, or `2`.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directness | Surrogate or different population/construct | Partially direct | Direct test of exact claim |
| Bias control | Major unresolved threats | Some concerns | Strong controls and validated measurement |
| Consistency | Single result or important conflict | Broad direction consistent with exceptions | Replicated across independent data |
| Precision | Very small or unstable | Moderate information/uncertainty | Precise enough for intended conclusion |
| Transportability | Unknown or narrow | Tested in one additional context | Replicated across relevant settings |

Scores guide discussion; they are not added mechanically. A fatal limitation can cap confidence regardless of the total.

## Required synthesis statement

Every rated claim must include:

- the exact interpretation supported;
- what the rating does **not** establish;
- the most important limiting evidence;
- the result most likely to change the rating;
- the date and person/process responsible for the judgment.

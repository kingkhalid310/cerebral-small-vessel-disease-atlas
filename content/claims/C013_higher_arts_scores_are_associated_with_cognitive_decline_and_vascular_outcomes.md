# C013: Higher ARTS scores are associated with cognitive decline and vascular outcomes

- **Domain:** prognosis
- **Confidence:** moderate
- **Interpretation supported:** Multiple older-adult cohorts show associations with cognition and incident MCI, dementia, or stroke
- **Not established:** That the associations are mediated specifically by arteriolosclerosis or improve decisions beyond established predictors

## Evidence relationships

### Supports: S006/R017

- **Population/model:** 369 MAP/ROS, 72 MARS, and 244 ADNI non-demented participants
- **Result:** Higher ARTS related to two-year decline, especially perceptual speed
- **Weight:** moderate
- **Limitation:** Multiple testing and causal specificity concerns
- **Extraction:** abstract_plus_full_text_web_extracted

### Supports: S007/R018

- **Population/model:** 156 Rush and 906 ARIC participants
- **Result:** Higher ARTS associated with worse baseline and longitudinal executive/perceptual speed measures
- **Weight:** moderate
- **Limitation:** Clinical validation was not pathology validation
- **Extraction:** abstract_extracted

### Supports: S008/R019

- **Population/model:** 1226 diverse older adults
- **Result:** Higher score associated with incident MCI, dementia, stroke, and vascular risks
- **Weight:** moderate
- **Limitation:** Incremental utility and mediation remain uncertain
- **Extraction:** abstract_extracted

## Synthesis judgment

- **Dimension scores:** directness 2/2; bias control 1/2; consistency 1/2; precision 1/2; transportability 1/2.
- **Key limitation:** Predictor composition and residual confounding complicate causal interpretation
- **Most decisive next test:** External incremental-value and decision-curve studies with pathology-informed mediation
